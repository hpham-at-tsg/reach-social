Post 1 · Tue Nov 3, 11:30 AM · Research spotlight (kids)
What if therapy started with the activity your child actually wants to do?

A new randomized trial in Pediatrics (August 2026) followed 110 children with cerebral palsy, ages 8 to 14. About half received Participate-CP: 12 weekly sessions built around each child's own activity goals and the specific barriers getting in the way.

Compared with usual care, those kids made bigger gains on their own activity goals, moved at a higher average intensity, reported fewer barriers, and took part in community activities more often.

The takeaway for families: start with what your child wants to do, name what is in the way, and bring that list to your therapy team.

Save this for your next visit.

Source: Sakzewski L, et al. Pediatrics. 2026;158(2):e2025075162.

Post 2 · Tue Nov 10, 11:30 AM · What's new at Reach
Something new at Reach: we are part of an NIH study testing the Agilik, a wearable exoskeleton made by Bionic Power to help children walk more easily and confidently.

Who can join: children ages 3 to 17 with conditions such as cerebral palsy, muscular dystrophy, spina bifida, or incomplete spinal cord injury who can walk at least 10 feet, with or without assistive support.

What it involves: a custom-fit device, about 9 months, up to 19 visits, and an option to use the device at home for part of the program. All visits, testing, and equipment are provided at no cost.

The study is run with Infinite Technologies Orthotics and Prosthetics in Arlington. Details at reachpedsrehab.com/agilik-research-study or call us at (571) 310-2502.

Post 3 · Tue Nov 17, 11:30 AM · Seasonal tips
Traveling for Thanksgiving? A little planning this week makes the trip easier for everyone.

1. Flying? Contact TSA Cares at least 72 hours before your flight at (855) 787-2227 or through the request form on tsa.gov. They can arrange help at the security checkpoint for travelers with disabilities and medical conditions.

2. Car seats: the American Academy of Pediatrics advises against puffy coats or snowsuits under the harness. They compress in a crash and leave the straps loose. Buckle in snugly, then put the coat or a blanket on top.

3. Keep medications, braces, and device chargers in your carry-on, not in checked bags.

4. Long drive? Plan stops to stretch, reposition, and move every couple of hours.

Send this to someone hitting the road next week. Happy Thanksgiving from all of us at Reach.

Post 4 · Tue Nov 24, 11:30 AM · Research spotlight (adults)
Ice season is coming. One of the best-studied ways to stay on your feet is also one of the simplest: practice your balance.

A Cochrane review pooled randomized trials of exercise for older adults living in the community. Balance and functional exercise reduced the rate of falls by about 24% (39 trials, 7,920 people, high-certainty evidence). Programs that combined balance and functional work with strength training likely did even better, about 34% fewer falls.

What counts: exercises that challenge your balance and mimic daily movements, like standing up from a chair, stepping, and turning, done regularly over months.

Not sure where to start, or living with a condition that affects your balance? Ask your physiatrist or physical therapist which program fits you.

Save this and share it with someone you love.

Source: Sherrington C, et al. Exercise for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2019;1:CD012424.
